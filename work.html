Rubric Data Pipeline & AI-Assisted Annotation

Ant Group · Alipay, Hangzhou · 2025.07 – 2026.01

Problem

Medical dialogue data has no agreed standard for "empathetic", no way to score it consistently between annotators, and no path from a score back into training. Three projects ran in sequence to close that loop: an annotation-assistance system, a three-tier quality-inspection system, and a Rubric-based data paradigm that turns evaluation into a training signal.

AI-assisted annotation system

A LangChain/ReAct agent over a structured workflow automates medical text annotation, fusing medical RAG with the LLM so that guideline context is retrieved before a label is proposed.

  • Annotation accuracy improved 40% against the manual baseline.
  • Processing throughput improved 50%.

Three-tier quality inspection

A three-stage pipeline automates inspection end to end — collection, annotation, then acceptance — with an SFT-tuned model performing multimodal fact verification at the final tier.

  • Data acceptance accuracy improved 50%.
  • Closed the data loop for downstream business training and product launch.

Rubric data paradigm

An end-to-end system for human-in-the-loop data production and model optimisation targeting improved empathy in medical conversations, solving three problems at once: standards that were hard to unify, evaluation that was hard to quantify, and training that was hard to align. The pipeline runs expert alignment, dynamic rubric generation, GPT-assisted query rewriting for the human-in-the-loop loop, and LLM-as-judge pairwise ranking — producing rubrics automatically, iterating the evaluation model, automating SFT-corpus polishing, and selecting RL queries. The resulting rubrics are then reused directly as the reward signal for RL training.

  • SFT model: +2.24 points on the offline empathy benchmark.
  • RL model: +5.63 points on the same benchmark.
  • Online A/B: thumbs-up/down ratio improved from 6.5% to 13%.
  • Resulting model versions reached full rollout.

SmartMapNet — Online Vectorized HD Map Perception

Smart Auto · 2024.06 – 2025.06

Setup

  • Sensors: 7 surround-view cameras plus LiDAR.
  • Platform: Nvidia Orin X.

Approach

A multi-task static-perception framework producing an online vectorized HD map, covering lane lines, parking-space lines, low-lying static obstacles and traffic-sign detection. It is built on MapTR and splits into an encoder and a decoder.

  • Encoder: image feature extraction with ResNet50, plus BEV feature encoding via LSS.
  • Decoder: Transformer-based, built from stacked self-attention and cross-attention layers.
  • Supervision: BEVSeg and PVSeg auxiliary losses sharpen semantic understanding.
  • Low obstacles: a dedicated regression head using spatial polygons with a two-stage matcher, because low obstacles are poorly served by the default quad representation.
  • Geometry: dynamic task sampling balances data distribution across tasks; VDDL (vector direction difference loss) fixes the line twisting seen in curves and turns.
  • Deployment: PTQ post-training quantization limits the accuracy lost from FP32, and Kalman filtering fused with odometry stabilises tracking and pose estimation.

Challenges

Non-homologous training data; fisheye projection into LSS; spatial quadrilateral regression; and the long tail of vehicle corner cases.

Outcome

Closed-loop vehicle-side testing completed; shipped on the AVP and LP programs.

Parking_OneNet — IPM Parking Perception

Smart Auto · 2022.09 – 2024.09

Setup

  • Imaging: four fisheye cameras covering front, rear, left and right, combined by IPM inverse perspective mapping into a surround bird's-eye view.
  • Platform: Nvidia Orin N.

Approach

A single multi-task 2D network over the four fisheye bird's-eye views, trained with a differential multi-scale feature fusion framework. Two target families get different treatment:

  • In-space static obstacles (wheel stops, ground locks) use a rotated-box detector regressing both position and orientation. Angle prediction is improved with KLD loss and PSC encoding.
  • Walls and columns use a keypoint-polygon detector regressing the ground contact point, with RANSAC applied to the network's point-set output to recover a more accurate contact point.

Vehicle-side post-processing

  • Parking-space AB points correct wheel-stop orientation; parking-space depth corrects wheel-stop pose.
  • A corner-rotation algorithm adapts wheel-stop orientation in tilted out-of-vehicle scenarios, handling the dual-wheel-stop case.
  • History-frame weighted fusion removes box jitter at IPM seams.
  • Kalman-filter motion modelling compensates for apparent wheel-stop motion caused by image stretching during parking.

Challenges

Wide real-world variation in wheel stops and ground locks; walls and columns easily confused with other scene elements; severe stretching in IPM output and the resulting ground-plane estimation error; and target jitter at IPM seams.

Outcome

APA, RPA and RSPA reached production with continued OTA iteration. Parking success exceeded 95% in open scenes, and AVP completed closed-loop vehicle integration.

Smart Parking Lot — Rotated Object Detection at the Edge

Dahua Technology · 2021.07 – 2021.11

Setup

  • Imaging: oblique cameras at 6 m, 12 m and 20 m covering indoor parking areas at airports, ports and expressway service stations.
  • Platforms: HiSilicon 3559A, Cambricon 220, Ascend, Atlas 310.

Approach

  • Mosaic augmentation for sample diversity, plus a sliding-window scheme that expands the count of each vehicle class on purpose.
  • YOLOv5 with a ring-shaped smooth loss for angle prediction, and ShuffleNet swapped in as the backbone to fit the edge budget.
  • Result: a rotated object detector that became part of the company's core algorithm library.

Outcome

Capture rate 95.56% and accuracy 95.80% — up 0.5 and 1.0 points over baseline.

Airport Turnaround Node Event Detection

Dahua Technology · 2021.11 – 2022.09

Setup

  • Imaging: 8 MP and 4 MP cameras at apron, side and jet-bridge interior positions.
  • Platforms: Tesla T4, Cambricon 220.

Approach

  • Cutout and Albumentations augmentation, plus offline sampling to raise the frequency of rare targets.
  • An added small-object detection head on YOLOv5 improves small-target feature learning, and a second-stage recognition pass lifts recall on fuel-hose, wheel-chock and similar small objects.
  • Detected aircraft, aircraft components, jet-bridge state and service vehicles feed a motion-state model based on IoU matching rules; events are then encoded as logic status codes and reported per turnaround node.

Outcome

Two airport projects won; algorithm competitive evaluations passed at several airports.
